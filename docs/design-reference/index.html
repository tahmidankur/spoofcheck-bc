Can someone forge email from a B.C. public body?

Type the part after the @ in their staff email addresses. You'll see what their DNS records tell other mail servers to do with a fake.

Your browser asks a public DNS service (dns.google) for the records. Nothing is sent to the domain itself.

What to fix, in order

  1. Refuse forged mail instead of filing it as spam Low

    Reports from the last few weeks should show which services send as this domain. Once they all pass, change the policy.

    _dmarc.riverbend.example  TXT  "v=DMARC1; p=reject; rua=mailto:dmarc@riverbend.example"

  2. Leave room under the SPF lookup limit Info

    Seven of ten lookups are used. Adding one more mailing service with nested includes could break SPF for every message.

  3. Ask senders to require encryption Info

    There's no MTA-STS policy. This is about privacy in transit, not spoofing, so it can wait until the DMARC change is done.

How the check works

  1. Once a week, the scanner asks DNS for each domain's DMARC, SPF, MX, MTA-STS and TLS reporting records, plus a few common DKIM keys.
  2. It reads the DMARC policy to set the level, and checks SPF for the mistakes that make it fail outright.
  3. Totals by sector are published here. Results for individual bodies are sent to them privately, not posted.

What it can't tell you

  • Whether a body's mail filters catch forged mail sent to its own staff. That happens inside their systems.
  • Whether DKIM is set up, if they use a selector name we didn't try.
  • Anything about websites, passwords or networks. Only public email records are read.
